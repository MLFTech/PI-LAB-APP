Pi Lab is a learning website about the Buckingham Pi theorem, built as a final year project. This page explains what Pi Lab stores about you, why, and what you can do about it.

What Pi Lab stores

  • When you sign in with Google: your Google account ID, email address and name. Pi Lab never sees or stores your Google password.
  • When you send the feedback questionnaire: your name, occupation, programme or field (if you give one) and your answers.
  • A sign-in cookie that keeps you signed in for up to two weeks, and a security cookie that protects forms. There are no advertising or tracking cookies.
  • The guide, exercises and calculator work without signing in, and nothing you type in the calculator is saved.

Why

  • Your answers are used only to evaluate Pi Lab as part of the final year project (for example, the average usability score).
  • Your email address is used only to make sure each person answers the questionnaire once.

Who can see it

  • Only the project's author{% if contact %} ({{ contact }}){% endif %}, and the project supervisor and examiners if they ask, through a password-protected admin page.
  • Results are reported as totals and averages. Your name and email are never published or shared, and are not sold or used for advertising.

How long it is kept

  • Until the final year project has been assessed. After that the responses and accounts are deleted.

Your choices

  • Answering the questionnaire is voluntary. You can stop at any time before pressing Send.
  • You can ask for your response and account to be deleted{% if contact %} by emailing {{ contact }}{% endif %}.
  • You can also remove Pi Lab's access to your Google account at any time at myaccount.google.com/connections.
